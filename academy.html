Keeping young women in triathlon.

The research is clear that girls are not choosing to leave. They're leaving because of a lack of support through puberty, a fear of being judged, and almost no honest education about how a female body actually trains, fuels and recovers.

And the ones who survive all that hit a second wall. In triathlon, the years between 18 and 25 are where the pathway quietly narrows to elite or nothing — research on the junior-to-senior transition suggests only around a third of junior athletes carry on into senior sport at all. If you're not being selected, and you're not ready to stop, there's very little built for you.

And for those who do stay, the risks change rather than disappear. Under-fuelling, disrupted or absent periods, stress fractures, chronic fatigue — often quietly normalised as the price of being committed. It isn't. RED-S (relative energy deficiency in sport) is one of the most under-discussed risks in triathlon, and it disproportionately affects exactly this age group.

My PhD research is in endurance performance and nutrition, and RED-S is one of the areas I've published on. This programme exists because that knowledge shouldn't sit in a journal that the people who most need it will never read.

Why it matters to me

I'm building the group I wish I'd had.

I was a triathlete from the age of eight. I was always the one complimented for being committed — never the one winning races.

As the years went on, I had fewer and fewer girls to race against. They either went international or disappeared from the sport altogether. What was left was training with groups of boys who didn't talk to me, or older men who'd drop me on rides and not look back. It was lonely in a way that had nothing to do with how hard I was training.

Eventually I fell out of love with it. During the pandemic I stopped triathlon completely and just ran instead.

I'm finding my way back now. And I want to build the group I would have loved to be part of when I was that age — where being the committed one is enough, where you're not the only woman in the session, and where nobody has to choose between going elite and going home.

How the community works

A few ground rules

  • This is a space to talk about training, not bodies. We keep the focus on what you're working towards and how it's going.
  • Questions about fuelling, periods, injuries or feeling tired are always welcome — that's a lot of why this group exists.
  • Anything you'd rather not raise with everyone, message me directly. Always.
  • Everyone here is training toward something different. Nobody's progress is the benchmark for anyone else's.
  • Be the person you'd have wanted in your group when you were starting out.

Given why this programme exists, how we talk to each other matters as much as the training itself.
